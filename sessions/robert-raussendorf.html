Quantum Computers and Cryptography

Robert Raussendorf

Recording

This session was recorded but consent to publication from the presenter has not yet been received.

About the 2014 talk

Three excursions into quantum computation. From Robert's abstract: “The field of quantum computation started in the 1980s with philosophy-minded people asking questions like: if quantum mechanics is the fundamental theory of the physical world, then shouldn't it also affect the theory of computation? Then, in 1994 came an enormous breakthrough: Peter Shor figured out an efficient quantum algorithm to decompose large integer numbers into their prime factors. The potential implications are not philosophical at all: Shor's algorithm breaks the cryptography system RSA, which is in widest use today for encrypting email and protecting credit card transactions.”

“It's pretty rugged and uncharted territory still, and I would like to take you on three excursions into this wild land: (i) the circuit model of quantum computation, leading up to Shor's algorithm; (ii) a different model, the adiabatic model — as many of you know, a Burnaby-based company, D-Wave, is dedicated to building such a device; and (iii) quantum error-correction — quantum computations need to be protected from decoherence, and I give you a glimpse into how this is done.” In Ray's words: “He has the rare skill of being able to speak about a complex subject so that the rest of us can understand and appreciate it.” Robert returned in October 2025 for a second session, on quantum computers and cryptography.
