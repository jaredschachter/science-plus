Bayesian Statistics

Larry Wasserman

Recording

This session was held in person, before Science Plus moved online, and was not recorded.

Returning November 24, 2026

Larry presents to Science Plus a second time on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, on what statistics is, what it is used for, and how AI has influenced it. Session details →

About this session

Professor of Statistics and Data Science at Carnegie Mellon University, where he holds appointments in the Department of Statistics and the Machine Learning Department and is a UPMC University Professor. Born in Windsor, Ontario, he took his BSc and PhD at the University of Toronto before joining Carnegie Mellon in 1988.

One of the most honoured statisticians of his generation. He received the COPSS Presidents’ Award in 1999, given each year to the outstanding statistician under forty, the CRM-SSC Prize in 2002, and was elected to the National Academy of Sciences in 2016. His textbook All of Statistics: A Concise Course in Statistical Inference won the DeGroot Prize and has introduced a generation of students and data scientists to the field; he is also the author of All of Nonparametric Statistics.

A session on Bayesian statistics — reasoning about an uncertain world by updating what you believe as the evidence arrives. Larry worked through examples contrasting the Bayesian and frequentist approaches to inference, making the case for where the Bayesian view has the edge. Writing to him afterwards, Ray wished the talk had been taped and asked him to set the examples down in a few paragraphs so Science Plus would have a record of them.
